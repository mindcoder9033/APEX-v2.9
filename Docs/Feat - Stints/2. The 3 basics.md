## 2. Allocate Maximum 3 Skills

These are the three fundamental problems, now framed with the specific terminology from Chapter 2.

1.  **Skill 1: Radius Optimization (The Line)**
    - **Focus:** Training the user to find the "Constant Radius Arc" or an "increasing radius arc" that allows for the highest possible speed. The goal is to use every inch of the road (Turn-in, Apex, Track-out).
    - **Feedback:** The "Arc Radius" gauge and "Apex Predictor" will provide immediate feedback on whether the user is early apexing (which tightens the radius) or hitting the geometric line.
    - **Relevant Chapter Text:** *"To find out how much faster you can go through Turn 7 on the right line... the radius of the arc that represents the racing line... is a whopping 195 feet. That's 89% bigger than the inside arc." (Page 19)*

2.  **Skill 2: Throttle & Cornering Balance (Exit Speed)**
    - **Focus:** Training the user on how throttle application affects the car's balance (understeer/oversteer) at the exit. The aim is to avoid "trailing throttle oversteer" and apply power smoothly to maximize exit speed.
    - **Feedback:** The "Throttle Balance Map" will show the consequences of abrupt throttle inputs, helping the user practice the "squeeze" technique.
    - **Relevant Chapter Text:** *"A gradual increase in throttle will tend to create understeer. An abrupt application of throttle will tend to create oversteer." (Page 27)*

3.  **Skill 3: Threshold & Trail Braking (Braking and Entering)**
    - **Focus:** Training the user on the "Brake and Turn" (trail braking) phase and maximizing deceleration without locking the wheels.
    - **Feedback:** The "Brake Performance Pulse" and "Brake Bias Indicator" will help the user find the maximum deceleration point and learn to blend braking with turning.
    - **Relevant Chapter Text:** *"The goal is to stay at this maximum traction point... any less pressure on the brake pedal, and less than the full 500 lbs. of braking traction would be used... at a particular level of pressure on the brake pedal you're right there at the peak. We call it the threshold." (Page 30)*

## 3. Allocate 3 Practice Stints (Stepping Stones)

These stints are designed to build the skills incrementally, mirroring the "make time" advice from the chapter (Page 25).

| Stint | Focus Area | Description & Guide | Key Quote from Chapter 2 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stint 1: The Line Hunter** | **Line Finding (Late Apex Strategy)** | **Goal:** Learn the line using the "Late Apex" strategy. Intentionally turn in later than you think, then gradually move the turn-in point earlier. The HUD's "Apex Predictor" will help you identify the true geometric line. | *"Since the late apex is a safer path to take through the corner, it's a very useful tool to use... When you feel you should turn in, overrule your instinct and turn later." (Page 25)* |
| **Stint 2: The Throttle Squeeze** | **Corner Exit Balance** | **Goal:** Experiment with throttle application at the corner exit. The focus is on the "pause" and "recovery" when dealing with oversteer. Practice smooth, gradual throttle application. | *"A gradual increase in throttle will tend to create understeer... You can load or unload the front or rear tires (change their traction) by applying or backing off the throttle." (Page 26)* |
| **Stint 3: The Brake Maestro** | **Threshold & Trail Braking** | **Goal:** Learn the feel of threshold braking. Practice the transition from straight-line braking to turning (trail braking) using the "Brake Performance" HUD. | *"To pin it down to a fundamental rule (remember, this is a guideline, not a guarantee): A gradual increase in throttle will tend to create understeer. An abrupt application of throttle will tend to create oversteer." (Page 27)* |

## 5. Suggest Game Type

- **Suggestion:** **Circuit Race**
- **Rationale:** The chapter focuses on minimizing *time* around a *circuit*. A Circuit Race provides a standard, repeatable environment where the user can practice the "line" and see immediate results in lap times.

---

## 6. Time of Day

- **Suggestion:** **Late Morning (10:00 AM)**
- **Rationale:** The chapter emphasizes finding "reference points" (Page 21-22). Late morning provides strong, directional light that creates clear shadows, making it easier to spot curbs, cracks, and paint lines on the track, which are essential for consistent line-finding.

---

## 7. Weather

- **Suggestion:** **Clear**
- **Rationale:** The chapter discusses the physics of grip and traction in a dry environment. Adding rain/wet conditions introduces a variable that changes the tire's coefficient of friction (G), which makes it difficult to learn the fundamental principles of radius, turn-in, and braking as described in the text.

---

## 8. Number of Laps

- **Suggestion:** **10-15 Laps**
- **Rationale:** This allows enough time to complete the three stints (approx. 3-4 laps each) with a couple of warm-up laps. This structure mimics the book's advice to "make time" and treat practice as a series of experiments.

---

## 9. Number of Drivatars

- **Suggestion:** **0 Drivatars**
- **Rationale:** The chapter's advice on "Charting a Lap" and "The Analytical Racer" (Page 33) emphasizes self-analysis. Other cars on the track are a distraction when trying to find "reference points" (cracks in the pavement, curbs) and trying to hit the "turn-in, apex, and track-out" points.
