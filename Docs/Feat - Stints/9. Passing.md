Based exclusively on **Chapter 9: Passing**, this tool is designed to teach the art and science of overtaking—from understanding the "rights to the line" and drafting dynamics to executing out-braking maneuvers and defending position fairly. The focus is on developing racecraft, sportsmanship, and the strategic thinking required to pass cleanly.

## 1. Features, UI/UX HUD (The Racecraft Analyst)

The HUD is built around visualizing passing opportunities, drafting benefits, and the critical "rights to the line" rules of engagement.

### Core UI/UX Design Philosophy
- **Situational Awareness:** The interface helps the driver understand their position relative to competitors and the rules that apply.
- **Draft Visualization:** Showing the aerodynamic benefit of following another car and when to pull out for the pass.

### HUD Features (In-Car Overlay)

| Feature | Description | How It Maps to Chapter 9 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **"Pass Rights Indicator"** | A visual judge showing the relative position of cars at the brake point. If the user is alongside (≥50% overlap), it flashes "HAS RIGHTS." If not, it shows "YIELD." | Implements the **"Rights to the Line"** section (Page 141-142). Teaches when the overtaken car must yield and when the overtaking driver is at fault. |
| **"Draft Benefit Gauge"** | A real-time display showing the speed advantage (in mph) gained from drafting the car ahead. It shows the benefit increase as the user gets closer. | Teaches **"How Drafting Increases Speed"** (Page 145). Shows the "relative vacuum" effect and the speed gain (e.g., +5 mph at 600' marker). |
| **"Closing Rate Monitor"** | A speed-sensitive indicator showing the closing rate between cars. If the rate is increasing rapidly, a warning flashes: "WATCH CLOSING RATE." | Teaches the critical **"Watch the Changing Closing Rate"** section (Page 147). Prevents contact when pulling out of the draft. |
| **"Out-Braking Distance"** | A distance marker showing how many feet later the user braked vs. the car ahead. If the user braked 20 feet later, a "CAR LENGTH GAINED" message appears. | Quantifies the **"How Deep is Deep?"** section (Page 152). 20 feet later = ~1 car length advantage at Sebring Hairpin. |
| **"Defensive Line Indicator"** | A track map overlay showing if the user is taking the inside line to defend position (green) or weaving/blocking (red). | Teaches the difference between **"Driving Defensively"** (legal) and **"Blocking"** (illegal/dirty) (Page 152-153). |

## 2. Allocate Maximum 3 Skills

These skills are derived from the chapter's focus on racecraft: passing rules, drafting, out-braking, and defense.

1.  **Skill 1: Rights to the Line & Passing Judgment**
    - **Focus:** Training the driver to correctly assess if they have the right to the line based on their position relative to the car ahead at the brake point and turn-in point.
    - **Feedback:** The "Pass Rights Indicator" evaluates if the driver attempts to pass when not alongside. If the driver forces a pass from behind and contact occurs, the system flags "At Fault."
    - **Relevant Text:** *"If, at the brake point, your car is dead alongside... the overtaken car is obliged to leave room for you. If you're trying to pass but you're not quite alongside... it's your responsibility to get out of the way." (Page 141)*

2.  **Skill 2: Draft-by Execution**
    - **Focus:** Training the driver to use the aerodynamic draft to gain speed and execute a clean pass on a straight.
    - **Feedback:** The "Draft Benefit Gauge" and "Closing Rate Monitor" guide the driver through the phases: Leave room → Close → Pull out → Complete the pass.
    - **Relevant Text:** *"The draft-by pass involves the driver behind using this speed advantage to close on and pass the car ahead... A successful draft-by pass starts before the corner leading onto the straight." (Page 145-146)*

3.  **Skill 3: Out-Braking Precision**
    - **Focus:** Training the driver to brake later than the car ahead to complete a pass in the braking zone without overslowing or missing the apex.
    - **Feedback:** The "Out-Braking Distance" measures how many feet later the user braked. The system warns if the driver over-slows (minimum speed too low) or misses the apex.
    - **Relevant Text:** *"Being able to threshold brake at the last possible moment when you want to or need to is possibly one of the most important skills for racing. It's the way you end up doing a lot of your passing." (Page 148)*

## 3. Allocate 3 Practice Stints (Stepping Stones)

| Stint | Focus Area | Description & Guide | Key Quote from Chapter 9 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stint 1: The Rights Learner** | **Rights to the Line** | **Goal:** Practice approaching corners with a Drivatar ahead. The HUD's "Pass Rights Indicator" will show if you are alongside. Only attempt a pass when the HUD shows "HAS RIGHTS." If not, yield and follow through the corner. | *"If the overtaking driver is not at least alongside at the turn-in point and there is contact... the overtaking driver is at fault." (Page 142)* |
| **Stint 2: The Draft-By Specialist** | **Drafting & Passing on Straights** | **Goal:** Follow a Drivatar closely on a long straight. Use the "Draft Benefit Gauge" to see the speed gain. Practice leaving 1-2 car lengths, closing, and smoothly pulling out to pass. Focus on timing the pull-out to avoid contact. | *"You want to time it so that you come off the corner faster and get a run down the first third or half of the straight, never having to lift off the throttle." (Page 146)* |
| **Stint 3: The Out-Braker** | **Passing in the Braking Zone** | **Goal:** Approach a high-speed braking zone (e.g., Sebring Hairpin) behind a Drivatar. Practice braking **later** than the Drivatar. Aim for a 20-foot (1 car length) later brake point. Focus on maintaining control and making the apex. | *"Going 20 feet deeper is going to take a lot of work and skill... the 20-foot difference in the brake point can mean the difference between winning and finishing second." (Page 152)* |

## 4. Suggest Suitable Car and a Track

- **Suggested Car:** **2014 BAC Mono**
    - **Rationale:** The chapter's passing examples (Formula Dodge) and drafting mechanics (low-horsepower cars with high aerodynamic drag) are best replicated in an open-wheel, low-horsepower track car. The BAC Mono is lightweight, has high aerodynamic drag, and provides excellent visibility for judging passing gaps—critical for the "rights to the line" drills.

- **Suggested Track:** **Sebring International Raceway (Full Circuit)**
    - **Rationale:** The chapter explicitly uses the **Sebring Hairpin (Turn 10)** for the out-braking example (Page 152). It also references the long straights for drafting and the esses for position fighting. Driving the same track allows the user to directly apply the chapter's "20 feet later = 1 car length" math.

---

## 5. Suggest Game Type

- **Suggestion:** **Circuit Race**
- **Rationale:** Passing is fundamentally a *race* skill, not a practice or time-trial skill. A Circuit Race provides the context of competitors, positions, and the pressure of racing, which is essential for practicing passing techniques and defensive driving.

---

## 6. Time of Day

- **Suggestion:** **Late Morning (10:00 AM)**
- **Rationale:** The chapter emphasizes the importance of **mirror usage** for situational awareness (Page 143). Late morning provides clear visibility in mirrors without glare, making it easier to judge closing rates and the position of cars behind.

---

## 7. Weather

- **Suggestion:** **Clear**
- **Rationale:** The chapter's passing techniques (drafting, out-braking, rights to the line) assume a dry track with consistent grip. Rain reduces overall grip, changes braking points, and introduces a "luck" factor that distracts from learning the core passing principles and sportsmanship.

---

## 8. Number of Laps

- **Suggestion:** **15 Laps**
- **Rationale:** With 15 laps, the driver will encounter multiple passing opportunities per lap, providing enough repetition for:
    - 5 laps for Stint 1 (Rights Learning)
    - 5 laps for Stint 2 (Draft-By)
    - 5 laps for Stint 3 (Out-Braking)
    - Enough data to see trends in passing success rate.

---

## 9. Number of Drivatars

- **Suggestion:** **3-5 Drivatars**
- **Rationale:** The chapter explicitly discusses passing *other cars*. To practice the techniques described—rights to the line, drafting, out-braking—there must be other cars on track. A small field of 3-5 Drivatars provides realistic traffic without the chaos of a full 24-car grid, allowing focused practice.
