The HUD is built around the chapter's emphasis on visibility, hydroplaning prevention, and the "rim shot" rain line technique.

## 2. Allocate Maximum 3 Skills

These skills are derived from the chapter's focus on wet-weather adaptation: visibility, line selection, and car control.

1.  **Skill 1: Visibility Management**
    - **Focus:** Training the driver to maintain visibility in spray and rain—prepping the visor, using ventilation, and avoiding fogging.
    - **Feedback:** The "Visibility Score" tracks if the driver can see clearly enough to identify braking points and apexes. If visibility drops, the system advises slowing down or finding cleaner air.
    - **Relevant Text:** *"Obviously, first and foremost, you can't control your car if you can't see where you're going. Maintaining the ability to see comes even before car changes as a priority in wet driving." (Page 181)*

2.  **Skill 2: Rain Line Selection (The "Rim Shot")**
    - **Focus:** Training the driver to find the grippiest pavement by driving off the polished dry line, using the "rim shot" or squaring off corners.
    - **Feedback:** The "Rain Line Advisor" and "Grip Surface Indicator" guide the driver to areas with better traction. Success is measured by corner exit speed (tachometer check).
    - **Relevant Text:** *"The rim-shot method worked in our test... the average lap time around the outside was eight seconds per lap faster... Go off-line, however, and there is less spilled lubricant and the track surface is much more porous." (Page 186)*

3.  **Skill 3: Wet Car Control (Traction Management)**
    - **Focus:** Training the driver to adapt to reduced grip—smoother inputs, avoiding wheelspin, and using the tires' strengths (braking/accel over cornering).
    - **Feedback:** The "Traction Circle (Wet)" shows the reduced grip envelope. The system alerts if the driver exceeds the wet grip limit.
    - **Relevant Text:** *"In the rain, little things can catch you out... any of the mistakes that you normally make in your dry-weather racing are magnified... You have to try to be as smooth as you can with all the controls." (Page 189)*

## 4. Suggest Suitable Car and a Track

- **Suggested Car:** **2014 BAC Mono**
    - **Rationale:** The chapter's rain examples reference open-wheel cars (spray, rooster tails). The BAC Mono provides excellent visibility (for spotting puddles and grip changes) and is lightweight, making the reduced grip of wet conditions more manageable. It also allows for manual setup changes (brake bias, pedal adjustments) as described in the chapter.

- **Suggested Track:** **Lime Rock Park (Full Circuit)**
    - **Rationale:** The chapter explicitly uses **Lime Rock** as the primary example for the "rim shot" technique (Page 186-187). It describes the track's three fast sweepers, two long hairpins, and banked medium-speed corners, making it the perfect laboratory for practicing rain lines. The track's polished asphalt surface also makes the dry line particularly slippery in the wet, amplifying the benefit of the rim shot.

## 5. Suggest Game Type

- **Suggestion:** **Circuit Race**
- **Rationale:** The chapter discusses wet-weather racing in a competitive context—traffic, spray, and the psychological challenge of driving in the rain. A Circuit Race provides realistic conditions (other cars, spray, the pressure of competition) that are essential for practicing wet-weather skills.

## 6. Time of Day

- **Suggestion:** **Late Morning (10:00 AM)**
- **Rationale:** The chapter mentions that oil and coolant are easier to spot "especially if the sun is low in the sky" (Page 175, Chapter 11). In wet conditions, late morning provides enough light to distinguish between dry patches, puddles, and polished surfaces, while avoiding the harsh glare that can make spray worse.

---

## 7. Weather

- **Suggestion:** **Heavy Rain (or Moderate Rain)**
- **Rationale:** The chapter is entirely about driving in rain. To practice the skills described—visibility management, rim shot line, squaring off corners, hydroplaning avoidance—the weather must be wet. Heavy rain provides realistic challenges (spray, puddles, reduced grip) that force the driver to adapt.

---

## 8. Number of Laps

- **Suggestion:** **15 Laps**
- **Rationale:** With 15 laps, the driver experiences:
    - 5 laps for Stint 1 (Visibility Drill)
    - 5 laps for Stint 2 (Rim Shot Hunting)
    - 5 laps for Stint 3 (Squaring-Off)
    - Enough data to compare lap times and exit speeds between different lines.

## 9. Number of Drivatars

- **Suggestion:** **10-12 Drivatars**
- **Rationale:** The chapter emphasizes the challenge of **spray** from other cars (Page 181). A full field provides realistic spray conditions, forcing the driver to manage visibility and adapt their line. It also creates traffic for practicing wet-weather passing and awareness.
