Based exclusively on **Chapter 7: Working Up a Track: From Map to Laps**, this tool is designed to guide the driver through a systematic, analytical approach to learning a new circuit—moving from first impressions to detailed corner analysis to on-track refinement. The focus is on the Skip Barber methodology of "analytical, methodological, technical approach" over raw adrenaline.

---

## 1. Features, UI/UX HUD (The Track Analyst)

The HUD is built around the three-step analysis process (Line → Exit Speed → Braking) and helps the driver track their progression from conservative pace to full attack.

### Core UI/UX Design Philosophy
- **Preparation over Reaction:** The tool guides the driver through the "homework" phase before they even turn a wheel.
- **Reference Point Tracking:** Visualizing the physical markers (curbs, seams, paint, cones) that are critical for consistency.

### HUD Features (In-Car Overlay)

| Feature | Description | How It Maps to Chapter 7 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **"Corner Priority Map"** | A mini-track map overlay showing each corner color-coded: **Type I** (Red - leads to long straight), **Type II** (Yellow - end of straight), **Type III** (Blue - leads to another corner). | Directly implements the **"Focus on the Details"** section (Page 107-108). Helps the driver identify which corners to compromise and which to maximize. |
| **"Reference Point Checklist"** | A list of stored reference points for the upcoming corner (Brake Point, Turn-in, Apex, Track-out) with visual markers on the track map. The driver can add/update these per lap. | Implements the **"Details, Details, Details"** approach (Page 103-104). The system tracks if the driver hits these marks consistently. |
| **"Turn-In Accuracy" Gauge** | A real-time gauge showing how close the car's actual turn-in point is to the planned reference point (e.g., "5 feet before striped curb"). | Teaches precision: *"We want to see our drivers get within 6 inches of the turn-in and track-out points."* (Page 22, Chapter 2) |
| **"Brake Point Progression" Tracker** | A lap-by-lap line graph showing the brake point distance from the corner. The driver can see if they are moving it closer (faster) or if the exit speed drops (too late). | Directly tracks **"The Procedure"** (Chapter 5) as applied to a specific corner. |
| **"Throttle Application Zone"** | A visual zone on the track map showing where the driver transitions from braking to full throttle. The HUD highlights if the driver is "patient" or "greedy" based on the corner type. | Reinforces the analysis from **"Turns 8 and 9"** (Page 117): *"You'll have to be patient here... the throttle application is likely to be a long progressive squeeze."* |

## 2. Allocate Maximum 3 Skills

These skills are derived from the chapter's systematic approach to learning a track: Line identification, Exit speed optimization, and Braking strategy.

1.  **Skill 1: Corner Analysis & Prioritization**
    - **Focus:** Training the driver to identify which corners are Type I (lead to straights), Type II (end of straights), and Type III (lead to other corners), and to determine the appropriate compromise strategy.
    - **Feedback:** Before each corner, the HUD displays the corner type. The system evaluates if the driver is sacrificing the correct corner (e.g., sacrificing Turn 8 for Turn 9).
    - **Relevant Text:** *"Since Turn 9 leads onto the longest period of full throttle acceleration on the racetrack, there is no doubt that you compromise the path through 8 in order to get to the right side of the road for the turn-in point for 9." (Page 116)*

2.  **Skill 2: Reference Point Precision (The "Sight Picture")**
    - **Focus:** Training the driver to use concrete, visible reference points (curbs, seams, paint, discolored pavement) to consistently hit the turn-in, apex, and track-out.
    - **Feedback:** The system tracks if the driver's turn-in point is within a 2-foot tolerance of the stored reference. It flags "Early Apex" if the driver turns in before the marker.
    - **Relevant Text:** *"Getting a consistent turn-in reference is going to be something you need to develop at a conservative pace... The apex reference for Turn 2 is easier; the concrete patch in the center of the corner makes a good target." (Page 109)*

3.  **Skill 3: The Speed Progression (Pacing)**
    - **Focus:** Training the driver to build speed incrementally—first learning the line, then adding corner exit speed, then optimizing braking—rather than going "10/10ths" on the first lap.
    - **Feedback:** A "Pacing Meter" shows the driver's speed relative to their planned progression (Warm-up → Building → Attack). If they exceed the attack pace in a warm-up lap, a warning appears.
    - **Relevant Text:** *"I'm going to go in there and I'm going to build up to it. I'm going to go a little bit—the heck with what they're saying back there with the stopwatches—I'm just going to go out there and do it at my own pace." – Mario Andretti (Page 120)*

## 3. Allocate 3 Practice Stints (Stepping Stones)

| Stint | Focus Area | Description & Guide | Key Quote from Chapter 7 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stint 1: The Reconnaissance Stint** | **Line & Reference Points** | **Goal:** Ignore lap time. Drive at 6/10ths. Focus *exclusively* on picking up reference points for turn-in, apex, and track-out. Use the HUD's "Reference Point Checklist" to log visual markers (seams, curbs, paint). | *"You can begin to identify likely reference points that will help you keep the car on the best path... Ninety percent of what you need to learn can be accomplished at less than 30 m.p.h." (Page 104)* |
| **Stint 2: The Exit Speed Builder** | **Throttle Application** | **Goal:** Once the line is established, focus on the throttle application point. Maximize corner exit speed by experimenting with earlier throttle application, being careful not to "over-greed" and delay the apex. | *"Since Turn 3 leads onto a long period of acceleration, so once you get the car turned in, you want to get to full power as soon as you can manage it, well before the apex." (Page 113)* |
| **Stint 3: The Brake Pointer** | **Brake Point Progression** | **Goal:** Apply "The Procedure" to each braking zone. Start conservative and move brake points closer by 3 feet per lap. If the driver misses the apex or delays throttle, move it back. | *"Expect to use threshold braking and to work the braking point toward the corner as you practice. Remember that getting to the turn-in point at the right speed is just as important as doing a perfect threshold braking level." (Page 119)* |

## 4. Suggest Suitable Car and a Track

- **Suggested Car:** **2014 BAC Mono**
    - **Rationale:** The chapter emphasizes a lightweight, open-wheel-style car for learning—the Formula Dodge. The BAC Mono replicates this: it's a single-seater, open-wheel track car with excellent visibility, allowing the driver to see reference points (curbs, seams) clearly. It has enough power to be rewarding but not overwhelming, perfect for the "analytical, methodological" approach.

- **Suggested Track:** **Sebring International Raceway (Full Circuit)**
    - **Rationale:** The ENTIRE chapter is a detailed analysis of this exact track. It describes the high-speed esses (Turns 1-2), the tight decreasing-radius Turn 6, the crucial Turn 7, and the most important corner—Turn 9 leading onto the longest straight. Driving the same track used in the textbook allows the driver to directly apply the written theory.

---

## 5. Suggest Game Type

- **Suggestion:** **Circuit Race (or Open Practice Session)**
- **Rationale:** The chapter emphasizes *practice* and *lap-by-lap progression*, not a single flying lap. A Circuit Race (Practice mode) allows unlimited laps to build speed gradually, test different lines, and experiment with brake points without the pressure of a Timed Race.

---

## 6. Time of Day

- **Suggestion:** **Late Morning (10:00 AM)**
- **Rationale:** The chapter stresses finding *visual* reference points: *"The apex reference for Turn 2 is easier; the concrete patch in the center of the corner makes a good target."* (Page 109). Late morning provides clear, high-contrast lighting to spot pavement seams, discoloration, and curbs.

---

## 7. Weather

- **Suggestion:** **Clear**
- **Rationale:** The chapter assumes a dry track for learning the *line* and *reference points*. Adding rain introduces variables (oil, water, reduced grip) that distract from the core task of identifying turn-in, apex, and track-out markers. The chapter also mentions "the marbles" (sand/grit) which are a dry-track hazard.

---

## 8. Number of Laps

- **Suggestion:** **20 Laps**
- **Rationale:** The chapter describes a process that requires *repetition*: *"This line development goes on lap after lap, always looking for better references and establishing what works best."* (Page 125). 20 laps allows for 3 distinct practice stints (approx. 6-7 laps each) with a warm-up and cool-down lap.

---

## 9. Number of Drivatars

- **Suggestion:** **0 Drivatars**
- **Rationale:** The chapter emphasizes focusing on *yourself* and *the track*. *"The competition at the front... is so close that the difference between winning and not is a matter of a tenth of a second or two per lap. This fact drives us to turn over every rock that may yield a lap time improvement."* (Page 103-104). Other cars obscure reference points and create unnecessary pressure during the learning phase.

#### Corner Analysis: Turn 9 (The Carousel) - Type I (Leads to Longest Straight)

**The Line:**
- **Your Plan:** Sacrifice Turn 8 to ensure you are on the right side of the road for Turn 9's turn-in. Aim for a *late apex* due to the decreasing radius.
- **Your Result:** Your turn-in point was **15 feet too early** on Laps 8-12. You corrected this on Laps 15-20.
- **Analysis:** The high curb obscured the apex, causing you to early-apex. This required a lift off the throttle on the straight, losing 4 mph.
- **Coaching:** *"It will be very easy to early apex this turn... It will be crucial to get a reference point so that you can check on your progress toward the apex in time to make an early correction." (Page 116-117)*
- **Action Item:** Practice Stint 1. Use the **pavement seam** 100 feet before the apex as a "waypoint" to check if you are inside or outside the correct path.

---

**Throttle Application:**
- **Your Plan:** *"The throttle application is likely to be a long progressive squeeze toward the floor... you still want to be hard in it before the apex, but not 100 feet before the apex."*
- **Your Result:** You went to 100% throttle **120 feet** before the apex on Laps 12-15, causing a push wide and a lift on exit. On Laps 18-20, you squeezed to 100% **60 feet** before the apex.
- **Analysis:** You were greedy on the throttle early. Patience is critical here.
- **Coaching:** *"If you go to a lot of power early... the car will gain too much speed by the apex and you'll almost certainly have to lift coming off the corner." (Page 117)*
- **Action Item:** Practice Stint 2. Focus on the *squeeze*. Delay full throttle until 50 feet before the apex. The straight is long enough—you don't need full power early.

---

**Braking:**
- **Your Plan:** Use threshold braking, but with a long trail-brake phase due to the decreasing radius. Brake in a straight line after Turn 8.
- **Your Result:** You were **too aggressive** on the brakes on Laps 5-10, locking the front right. You modulated better on Laps 15-20.
- **Analysis:** The car is not straight long enough between 8 and 9. You must make sure the car is settled before heavy braking.
- **Coaching:** *"Considering the fact that Turn 9 is such a long corner... you may want to use the big radius in the early part of the turn to carry extra speed in... a lot more brake-turning than straight-line braking." (Page 118)*
- **Action Item:** Practice Stint 3. Focus on *trail-braking* into Turn 9. Carry the brake past the turn-in, gradually releasing pressure.

### Overall Summary & Next Steps

1.  **Top Priority:** **Turn 9 Apex Precision**. You are early-apexing. Use the "waypoint" (pavement seam) to check your position 100 feet before the apex.
2.  **Next Priority:** **Throttle Patience**. Stop going to 100% power 120 feet before the apex. The straight is long—wait for the car to rotate.
3.  **Final Step:** **Trail-Brake Control**. Turn 9 is a decreasing radius corner. Blend braking and turning to carry more entry speed.
